# AI Pack LLC — Design Guidelines

This guide is here to help you get the best possible results out of AI Pack. The AI is powerful, but like any creative partner, it does its best work when you give it clear direction. Below you'll find practical advice on writing prompts, choosing styles, preparing artwork for print, and avoiding the common pitfalls that lead to disappointing designs.

## Writing a good prompt

The single biggest factor in a great design is the prompt. A vague prompt like "a cool shirt" gives the AI almost nothing to work with, so it guesses — and you usually won't love the guess. Instead, try to describe four things: the subject, the style, the mood or color palette, and any text.

For example, rather than "a dog," you might write "a golden retriever puppy sitting in a field of daisies, soft flat illustration style, warm pastel colors." That gives the AI a subject (the puppy), a setting (the field), a style (flat illustration), and a palette (warm pastels). The result will be far more focused and much closer to what you had in mind.

If you have text on your design, put it in quotes so the AI knows to render it exactly. For instance, the words "Adventure Awaits" in a bold retro font. Keep text short — long phrases are harder to render cleanly and tend to come out garbled.

## Iterate, don't restart

You rarely get the perfect design on the first try, and that's fine. The best approach is to generate your first four designs, pick the option that's closest to your vision, and then refine it step by step. You can ask for one change at a time — "make the background darker," "remove the extra text at the bottom," "give it a more hand-drawn feel" — and the AI will adjust while keeping the parts you liked. Small, specific requests work much better than rewriting the whole prompt from scratch.

## Choosing a style

AI Pack can produce a wide range of visual styles, and naming the style you want makes a huge difference. Some styles that tend to print beautifully include flat vector illustration, line art, watercolor, vintage or retro poster, minimalist, and bold graphic. Photorealistic images can look stunning on screen but sometimes lose detail when printed on fabric, so if you're making apparel, simpler and bolder usually translates better to the final product.

If you're not sure what style you want, try describing an artist or era you like ("1970s concert poster," "Japanese woodblock print," "modern flat corporate illustration") and see what comes back. You can always dial it in from there.

## Designing for the product, not just the screen

A design that looks great as a square image on your screen won't automatically look great on a hoodie or a mug. Keep the shape of the product in mind. Tall designs suit the front of a t-shirt; wide designs suit mugs and tote bags; simple centered graphics work almost everywhere. Our editor shows you a live preview on the actual product, so use it — move and resize your design until it sits well within the printable area.

## Backgrounds and transparency

For most apparel, you'll want a design with a transparent or clean background so it sits naturally on the garment color. If your design has a solid rectangular background, it will print as a rectangle on the shirt, which is usually not the look people want. You can ask the AI to "remove the background" or "make the background transparent," and you can preview how it looks on different garment colors before ordering.

## Colors and contrast

Think about the color of the item you're printing on. Light, pastel designs can disappear on white products, and dark designs can vanish on black ones. Aim for good contrast between your design and the product color. Our preview will show you roughly how it'll look, but as a rule of thumb, pick a garment color that makes your design pop rather than blend in.

## Text and readability

If text is central to your design, keep it large, bold, and high-contrast. Thin or decorative fonts can look elegant on screen but become hard to read once printed at a small size. Double-check spelling before you order — the AI renders the words you give it, and it won't catch a typo for you. Read every word on your final design carefully, because reprints for typos that were in the original design aren't covered.

## Resolution and print quality

For crisp prints, your design needs to be high resolution — the equivalent of at least 300 DPI at the size it'll be printed. Our AI generates artwork at print-ready resolution, and our system flags any design that would come out too small or soft before checkout. If a design ever looks low-res in the preview, generate a fresh version rather than trying to stretch it — enlarging a small image never adds detail; it only makes the blur bigger.

## What the AI struggles with

It helps to know the AI's weak spots so you can plan around them. Hands, fine text, and highly specific real-world logos or brand marks are notoriously tricky. Very complex scenes with lots of small elements can come out cluttered. If you need something precise — an exact logo, a specific typeface, a trademarked character — the AI is not the right tool, and reproducing protected material isn't allowed anyway. For those cases, our human design assist service can step in.

## Respecting other people's work

Please don't ask the AI to recreate copyrighted characters, brand logos, celebrity likenesses, or another artist's distinctive style for commercial use. Besides being against our AI Generation Policy, these designs can get your order rejected and can expose you to legal risk if you sell them. Original ideas are not only safer — they're usually more interesting anyway.

## A few example prompts to get you started

If you're staring at a blank prompt box, here are some that tend to produce strong results:

"A minimalist mountain range at sunrise, three flat color layers, muted earth tones, no text."

"A cute cartoon cat astronaut floating among stars, bold outlines, bright colors, sticker style."

"Vintage 1950s travel poster of a beach town, warm retro palette, the words 'Ocean Days' in a classic serif font."

"Hand-drawn botanical line art of eucalyptus leaves, single continuous line, black on transparent background."

Feel free to borrow these, swap out the details, and make them your own.

## When in doubt, ask us

If you've tried a few times and still aren't getting what you want, reach out to support. We're happy to suggest prompt tweaks, and for bigger projects our designers can step in directly. There's no such thing as a silly design question here — we'd much rather help you get it right than have you settle for something you don't love.
